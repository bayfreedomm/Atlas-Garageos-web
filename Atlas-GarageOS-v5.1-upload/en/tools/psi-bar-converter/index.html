Quick understanding

What matters here

Conversion is not a recommendation

32 PSI and about 2.21 bar describe the same pressure; neither tells you what your vehicle should use.

Cold pressure matters

Vehicle placard recommendations are normally intended for tires measured cold.

Pressure changes with temperature

A warm tire can show a higher pressure even when no air was added.

!
Common mistake

Using the maximum pressure molded on the tire sidewall as the vehicle's normal recommended pressure.

!
Important warning

Use the vehicle placard or manufacturer guidance for recommended tire pressure. This tool only converts units.

How it works

Three common pressure units

PSI is pounds per square inch. Bar and kilopascals are metric pressure units, with exactly 100 kPa in one bar.

The number you actually need isn't on the tire

The pressure printed on the tire's sidewall is the tire's maximum safe rating, not your car's recommended pressure. The correct number for your vehicle is on a sticker inside the driver's door jamb (or in the owner's manual) — this converter just moves that number between units once you have it. If you're also comparing a different tire size, the Tire Size Calculator shows how the physical dimensions change.

Keep in mind

This tool converts pressure units only. It does not recommend a vehicle-specific tire pressure; use the vehicle manufacturer’s sourced specification.
