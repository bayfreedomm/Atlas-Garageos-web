Accuracy & methodology

Atlas separates exact unit conversion, nominal geometry, user-entered estimates, and real-world uncertainty instead of turning every result into a false verdict.

What runs in your browser

Calculator inputs are processed on the device. Core calculations are deterministic functions: the same valid inputs produce the same result without an Atlas account or application server.

Tire geometry

Metric tire dimensions use nominal sidewall geometry: sidewall height = section width × aspect ratio; overall diameter = rim diameter in millimetres + two sidewalls; circumference = π × diameter. Actual mounted tires vary by product, rim width, pressure, load, wear, and manufacturing tolerance.

Wheel width & offset

Wheel-position tools compare the half-width change and offset change to calculate inner-edge movement and outer-poke movement. They describe static wheel geometry only — not brake shape, tire bulge, suspension travel, steering angle, alignment, or body clearance.

Fuel & trip estimates

Fuel tools use the distance, efficiency, price, trip direction, passenger count, and extras you enter. They do not predict traffic, weather, driving style, fuel-price changes, or a specific vehicle's future consumption.

Unit conversions

Pressure, power, torque, distance, and fuel-economy conversions use explicit unit standards. MPG calculations keep US and Imperial gallons separate because they are different volumes.

EV charging

Energy and cost calculations include the efficiency value you enter. Charging time is an idealized constant-power estimate; real charging can be limited by the vehicle, charger, battery temperature, state of charge, and charging curve.

Fitment status

Atlas does not treat a non-negative static clearance as proof of vehicle compatibility. When no user target exists, geometry should remain neutral rather than becoming a green road-safety verdict.

How to use Atlas

Use the calculators to understand direction, magnitude, and relationships before making a decision. For anything safety-critical, legal, load-related, or vehicle-specific, verify against the vehicle manual, component manufacturer documentation, and a qualified professional.
