Quick understanding

What matters here

Offset is not the whole story

Changing wheel width changes both inner and outer position even when ET stays the same.

Lower ET usually moves outward

For the same wheel width, reducing positive offset generally moves the wheel farther toward the fender.

Inner clearance can shrink quietly

A wider wheel may gain outer presence while also moving its inner edge closer to suspension components.

!
Common mistake

Comparing ET numbers without comparing wheel widths. An 8J ET35 and a 9J ET35 do not sit in the same place.

!
Important warning

Wheel position does not prove brake, tire, suspension, or fender clearance on a specific vehicle.

How it works

Two directions, one transparent comparison

A positive inner-position change means the new inner wheel edge moves farther inward. A positive outer-poke change means the outer wheel edge moves farther outward.

Use Measure My Car before interpreting either number as available clearance.

Keep in mind

Position changes are geometric. Actual clearance depends on your vehicle, tire shape, suspension, brakes, bodywork, alignment, and operating conditions.
