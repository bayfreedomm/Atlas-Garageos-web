Quick understanding

What matters here

Width and diameter are different

A wider tire can still have nearly the same — or even a smaller — overall diameter.

Ride-height change is half the diameter change

Only the radius sits between the hub and the road, so a 10 mm diameter increase raises static ride height by about 5 mm.

Speed follows rolling diameter

If calibration stays unchanged, a larger rolling diameter makes actual speed higher than the indicated speed.

!
Common mistake

Assuming a wider tire is automatically taller, safer, or better in wet conditions. Width alone cannot answer those questions.

!
Important warning

This is nominal geometry, not a wet/dry grip or fitment verdict. Tread design, compound, pressure, load, wheel width, and vehicle setup also matter.

How it works

What changes between the sizes

Positive diameter and circumference differences mean the new tire is larger. Ride-height difference is half of the diameter change. The speedometer percentage shows the theoretical change at the same wheel rotation rate.

No percentage is treated as an automatic compatibility or safety limit.

Keep in mind

This comparison describes nominal tire geometry only. It does not verify wheel clearance, vehicle compatibility, load rating, or road use.
